Cycle 094 — Non-Uniform Compression Allocation — GPT-2 FFN
Item: 110
Date: 2026-03-29
Verdict: REFUTED

Hypothesis tested: Allocating more channels to high-damage blocks (and fewer to low-damage blocks)
at matched total budget (2048 channels across 4 blocks) achieves ≥10% relative PPL improvement
over uniform 6× compression.

Result: Non-uniform allocation provides negligible benefit (+0.24% delta vs uniform).
All three allocations are statistically indistinguishable after 100-step Adam polish.

Metrics (3 seeds, OWT, baseline PPL=51.66±5.49):
  uniform_6x         (512/512/512/512):    PPL=69.40±9.69  (+34.3% vs baseline)
  mild_nonunif       (614/512/512/410):    PPL=69.56±9.64  (+34.6% vs baseline)
  aggressive_nonunif (768/614/358/308):    PPL=70.25±9.83  (+36.0% vs baseline)
  best nonunif delta vs uniform: +0.24% (REFUTED threshold: <3%)

Interpretation: The output-projection refit (lstsq) already exploits the block's actual
activations. The topk selection + lstsq initialization is insensitive to channel count
beyond a threshold — more channels in high-damage blocks do not help because the
damage comes from the compressed representation, not from channel count per se.
The 100-step Adam polish then levels the playing field regardless of initial allocation.
