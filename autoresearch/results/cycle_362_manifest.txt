learned=Cross-block topk error decorrelation analysis. Six blocks [b2, b1, b10, b4, b3, b11], k=512 topk+lstsq applied independently to each block, error matrices E_b (25600×768) computed. Pairwise cosine of flattened error vectors gives a 6×6 correlation matrix. Off-diagonal |cosine| values: mean=0.0162, max=0.0703, min=0.0003. All off-diagonal values are far below the REFUTED threshold of 0.1. **Verdict: REFUTED.** The topk per-block errors are essentially orthogonal across blocks — the errors are already decorrelated. Deliberate cross-block decorrelation in channel selection would not help because there is nothing to decorrelate. The composition failure (6-block PPL 65.54, 12-block PPL 186) is caused by something else. Per-block error spectral stats: eff_rank ranges from 419 to 518 (all near 768 max), top1_frac 0.029-0.068, Frobenius norms vary 4.5x across blocks (1385 to 6293) with b10 and b11 having the largest errors. The Frobenius variation suggests the problem: blocks differ hugely in how much error topk introduces per block (b11 Frobenius=6293 vs b1=1385, 4.5x). The large absolute error at some blocks (not cross-block correlation) is the likely driver of PPL collapse. **Constructive follow-up**: analyze whether Frobenius-weighted block ordering or adaptive k per block (more channels to high-error blocks) improves multi-block stacking. The orthogonality of errors is actually a positive finding — it means errors don't destructively interfere, they just accumulate additively, so the total error grows linearly with the number of blocks. Reducing per-block error magnitude (via more channels or better selection) is the right direction.
