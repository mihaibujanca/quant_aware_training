Substantive finding (the result the verbatim verdict cannot capture):
  Both datasets show post-training eff_rank <= 3 in 11/12 (spirals) and 12/12 (moons) layers,
  matching cycle 278 GPT-2 b1/b2 (1.94, 1.05) in shape. BUT the init clause fails because
  init eff_rank is ALREADY <= 3 in 12/12 layers for both datasets — and reaches 1.0 by layer 7.
  With a 2D input, no random Gaussian + ReLU stack can create new effective dimensions; the
  rank is inherited from the input and only contracted by ReLU. The collapse is therefore
  NOT training-induced on this testbed; it is INPUT-DIMENSION-induced.

  This corresponds to item 307's "CONFIRMED-init-rank" outcome category, applied to its
  prerequisite. Hanin & Rolnick's N^d bound (lit map line 594) is the right reference: with
  d=2, a depth-12 32-wide ReLU net cannot exceed eff_rank ~2 anywhere in the stack, training
  or no training. The cycle 278 GPT-2 b1 collapse to eff_rank 1.94 cannot be attributed to
  "generic implicit regularization on overparameterized nets" via this experiment, because
  the spirals/moons testbed has no opportunity to collapse — it starts collapsed.

  The 2D-input-bottleneck testbed is therefore the WRONG control for cycle 278's question.
  A controlled comparison would need an input dimension large enough that init eff_rank can
  exceed the post-training value (e.g. the 100D embedding from MEMORY.md, or a higher-dim
  task). Item 308 (SDF, 3D input, eff_rank target [2.5, 3.5]) and item 307 (eff_rank
  trajectory through training on spirals) both depend on item 305 in the existing program;
  item 307 in particular will hit the same input-dim bottleneck.

  Implication for item 305 follow-ups: rerun with a higher-dim embedding of spirals/moons
  (e.g. random 32D linear lift, MEMORY.md's 100D recipe) to get an init eff_rank that can
  meaningfully drop. Without that lift, item 305 cannot distinguish training-induced from
  init-induced rank collapse.
