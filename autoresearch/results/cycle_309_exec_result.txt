Geometric reading:
1. Δy is rank-1 dominated (91% top1, stable rank 1.20). But the rank-1 axis Vh_y[:1]
   captures only 1.27% of W_proj's Frobenius energy -- it lies in W_proj's *tail*.
2. The cycle 306 "53% unprojected" framing measures how much of Δy lives in span(Vh_w[:2])
   on the *output* side. Replacing c_proj's row space with span(Vh_y[:r]) on the
   *output* side is geometrically equivalent to setting c_proj_8.row_i := P_y row_i,
   where P_y projects R^768 columns onto the 1D or 2D Δy axis. This kills 98%+ of
   the W_proj operator and keeps only its projection onto a tiny subspace -- which
   happens to be where Δy lives by construction, but is *not* where the rest of
   c_proj's input-output mapping (the GELU portion) lives.
3. Both bases at this rank can be Adam-fit to the same neighborhood (~52.85--54.32),
   because the trainable factor B (r, 768) has full row freedom in R^768 and the
   bias (768,) absorbs translation. The frozen A_y vs A_w difference is what gates
   the *direction* the trainable parameters can search. wproj wins because A_w is
   a rank-r orthogonal-flavored projector (U_w[:, :r] * S_w[:r]) onto W_proj's
   highest-energy *input* directions, whereas A_y = W_proj @ Vh_y[:r].T is a
   rank-r projection onto Δy's specific output directions, which point into
   W_proj's *low-energy tail* on the input side.
4. Implication: cycle 306's "Δy alignment is the right reading at b8" is REFUTED
   at the comparative level. The b8 PPL gap of any rank-r LASER is determined
   by the *input-side* singular structure of W_proj, not by the *output-side*
   alignment with Δy. The +1.04 PPL margin wproj_r2 vs dy_r2 is small but
   robust across seeds (wproj_r2 std=0.013, dy_r2 std=0.004).
