== Substantive findings ==
1. **The item 309 dichotomy is too coarse.** Both proposed stories are wrong in their
   strong form. B(t) rotates SLOWLY and PARTIALLY out of the SVD row space — 11% drop
   in principal-angle cosine over 4000 steps, while PPL improves by 8.6. The rate is
   remarkably uniform: the cos trajectory is essentially linear in step count
   (0.9976 → 0.9855 → 0.9691 → 0.9555 → 0.9446 → 0.9340 → 0.9241 → 0.9179 → 0.9111).
   No plateau, no phase transition.

2. **PPL improvement tracks drift tightly.** corr(fro_rel, Δppl) = +0.958. The two
   quantities move together almost in lock-step: every 0.01 rise in fro_rel buys
   roughly ~0.2 PPL. If Adam kept running past step 4000, both quantities would almost
   certainly keep moving; the budget-bounded nature of cycle 280 is confirmed at the
   trajectory level.

3. **The bias is doing substantial work.** ||bias(t) - bias(0)|| reaches ~2.0 by step
   4000, which is about half the RMS of the weight delta (fro_rel 0.44 × ||B(0)||_F ≈
   0.44 × 39.3 ≈ 17, so weight delta ≈ 17 vs bias delta ≈ 2 — bias delta is not
   dominant but is non-trivial). A separate ablation (bias-frozen retrain) would be
   needed to determine how much of the 8.6 PPL recovery is the bias specifically, but
   the cycle 280 result (-8.6 PPL with B + bias) cannot be explained by row-space
   rotation alone.

4. **W(t) = A @ B(t) has SV ordering change**: S[0] at W(0) = 28.71 reflects the
   frozen column A[:, 0] = U[:, 0] * 28.71; at W(4000) the top-2 right SVs have rotated
   by ~26° (cos drop to ~0.90). Because A is frozen, the column space of W(t) is
   invariant — the rotation is in the row space and it is modest.

5. **Cycle 280 reference match**: our init PPL 57.6581 matches cycle 280's seed-42
   noretrain within 0.006; best PPL 49.0603 matches cycle 280's retrain best_mean
   49.032 within 0.028 (single-seed vs 3-seed mean); final@4000 49.2498 shows the
   familiar cycle 280 late-step noise (best was at 3900, step 4000 slightly worse).

6. **Geometric implication for the LASER recipe**: neither "eff_rank predicts the
   optimal rank-2 operator" (CONFIRMED stay-put) nor "eff_rank predicts only r"
   (CONFIRMED drift) is quite right. The truth in the middle: eff_rank predicts both
   r AND a reasonable basin (the SVD init is ~83% of the optimal rank-2 operator by
   principal-angle energy), and Adam fine-tunes both a small row-space rotation AND
   the bias to close the remaining ~17%. A pure closed-form SVD-LLM solution would
   land near 57.66 PPL (the init) — it would NOT match cycle 280's 49.03 without at
   least a bias fine-tune and some small row-space rotation. This is a weaker claim
   than "eff_rank is the answer" but stronger than "eff_rank is a starting point".
