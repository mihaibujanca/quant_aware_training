== Geometric object ==

For each pair (b_i, b_j):
  LowRankCProj_i:
    A_i = U_i[:, :1] * S_i[:1] in R^{3072 x 1}  (frozen buffer)
    B_i in R^{1 x 768}                          (trainable, init Vh_i[:1, :])
    bias_i in R^{768}                           (trainable, init W_proj_i.bias)
    W_i = A_i @ B_i                             in R^{3072 x 768}
    forward: x @ W_i + bias_i
  [same for block j]

Joint Adam over {B_i, bias_i, B_j, bias_j}; rest of model frozen.
Trainable per pair = 1*768 + 768 + 1*768 + 768 = 3,072.

At rank-1 each block has a SINGLE frozen left singular direction
(U_l[:, 0] in R^{3072}) through which its c_proj output reaches the
residual stream. At (b10, b11) the two frozen directions U_10[:, 0]
and U_11[:, 0] are the ONLY c_proj output directions available to
the joint Adam fit. If these two directions are near-parallel in
R^{3072}, the two blocks' outputs are geometrically indistinguishable
to the downstream layers, and joint Adam cannot produce independent
b10 and b11 contributions. The 70.01 PPL measured here is the Adam
basin under this constraint.

At cycle 310's heterogeneous ranks (r_10=5, r_11=22) the pair had
5 + 22 = 27 combined frozen left directions, giving Adam 27 degrees
of freedom to decouple b10 from b11. The 60.72 PPL at cycle 310
reflected this partial decoupling; the +9.30 PPL jump at rank-1 is
the price of removing 26 of those 27 degrees of freedom.

The auditor recommends the cycle 326 M1 mechanism test (cos(Delta_y_10,
Delta_y_11) per token) at rank-1 as the direct geometric verification:
if U_10[:, 0] . U_11[:, 0] has high magnitude (close to +/- 1), the
rank-1 destruction is explained by left-vector colinearity; if it is
near 0, the destruction is a higher-order interaction (LayerNorm
coupling, Adam saddle).
