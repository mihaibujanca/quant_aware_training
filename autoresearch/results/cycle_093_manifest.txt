## Summary

Tested whether reducing compression ratio (3× vs 4× vs 6×) lowers the per-block
compounding coefficient c in the multiplicative PPL model PPL(k) = a*(1+c)^k.

Result: c is ratio-independent. All three ratios converge to c≈0.41:
  - 3× (k=1024): c=0.406, R²=0.921
  - 4× (k=768):  c=0.404, R²=0.932
  - 6× (k=512):  c=0.408, R²=0.933

The null hypothesis is confirmed: the ~41%/block error compounding rate is intrinsic
to the topk+lstsq+polish pipeline and does not depend on compression ratio.

## Interpretation

Single-block compression quality does improve at lower ratios (better PPL at k=1), but
the improvement vanishes entirely when blocks are stacked. The compounding rate c≈0.41
is invariant across compression levels. This implies the bottleneck is not the
per-block output approximation error, but rather the cascade dynamics of stacking.
The stacking catastrophe is structural, not a function of compression depth.

6× sanity check passed: c=0.408 (cycle 089 reference: c≈0.41). ✓

Monotonicity (c_3x ≤ c_4x ≤ c_6x) is violated by Δ≈0.002 (0.406 vs 0.404 at 3×/4×),
within noise — not a bug.
