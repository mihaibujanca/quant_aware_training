== Substantive findings ==
1. **Item 309's dichotomy is too coarse.** Neither CONFIRMED-drift nor CONFIRMED
   stay-put fits. B(t) makes a SLOW, STEADY, PARTIAL rotation out of the SVD row
   space: 1.0 → 0.91 cos over 4000 steps, essentially linear in step count. The
   SVD init basin contains ~83% of the optimal rank-2 operator (cos² ≈ 0.83);
   Adam closes the remaining ~17% while also fine-tuning the bias.

2. **Drift tracks PPL improvement tightly.** corr(fro_rel, Δppl) = +0.958. The two
   quantities move in lock-step. Every 0.01 step of fro_rel buys roughly 0.2 PPL.
   There is no phase where cos moves but PPL doesn't, or vice versa.

3. **Bias is doing non-trivial work** but not dominant. ||bias(4000) - bias(0)|| = 2.0;
   compare ||B(4000) - B(0)||_F ≈ 17 (fro_rel 0.44 × ||B(0)||_F ≈ 39.3). Weight
   delta ~8.5x bias delta in Frobenius norm, but bias still moves a full 2 units
   from its factory value. A bias-frozen ablation would decompose the 8.6 PPL
   into (row-rotation, bias) contributions; item 309 did not ask for this.

4. **W(t) = A @ B(t) geometry**: column span invariant by construction (A frozen);
   row space rotates by ~26° over 4000 steps (acos(0.90) ≈ 26°). Top-1 SV of W(t)
   rises from 28.71 to 30.40 (+5.9%); top-2 SV drops from 23.10 to 22.78 (-1.4%).
   The operator is being "sharpened" on its dominant direction.

5. **Geometric implication for LASER**: eff_rank predicts BOTH r AND the basin
   (SVD init is 83% of optimal by principal-angle energy); Adam fine-tunes a
   small row-space rotation AND the bias to close the remaining gap. A closed-
   form SVD-LLM solution without bias fine-tuning would land at 57.66 PPL (the
   init); it would NOT match cycle 280's 49.03. This is weaker than "eff_rank
   is the answer" but stronger than "eff_rank is a starting point".

6. **Budget-bounded**: cos and fro_rel are still moving at step 4000 (no plateau);
   corr(fro_rel, Δppl) = 0.958 shows PPL would keep improving if Adam continued.
   Cycle 280's choice of 4000 steps is a budget, not a convergence point.
